Teaching a cat that the carrier is not a trap

Feline care. Written by Rosa Beltrán, CVT. Reviewed by Dr. Priya Raghunathan, DVM. Last updated 12 May 2026.

Most cats are not frightened of the veterinary clinic. They are frightened of the carrier, because the carrier lives in a closet for eleven months of the year and then appears on the one morning that ends in a stranger touching them. The cat is doing good pattern recognition. The fix is to break the pattern.

Leave it out permanently

Take the door off, put a blanket that already smells of your cat inside, and leave the carrier in a room the cat uses. Not the hallway, not the garage. Somewhere they nap. Feed treats near it for a few days without asking for anything, then inside it, then further inside.

Make the door mean nothing

Once your cat sleeps in it by choice, put the door back on and leave it open. Close it for three seconds while they eat, then open it. Build to thirty seconds over a week. If your cat freezes or bolts, you moved too fast, so drop back two steps rather than pushing on.

Move the carrier before you need to

Pick it up with the cat in it, walk to the front door, put it down, open it, let them leave. Then to the car. Then a two minute drive that ends back at home. Cats that have only ever been driven to a clinic learn that a moving car means a clinic.

If you only have four days

Skip to the treat and blanket stage, use a top loading carrier rather than a front loading one, and ask us for gabapentin. It is a well tolerated pre-visit medication, it takes about two hours to work, and it makes the difference between a cat that can be examined and one that cannot. Ask us at least three days before the appointment so we can dispense it and check it is appropriate for your cat.

This is general guidance.

It is not a diagnosis or a prescription. Medication decisions belong to a veterinarian who has examined your animal and knows their history.

What puppy socialisation actually means

Training. Written by Dana Whitcomb, CPDT-KA. Last updated 3 April 2026.

The socialisation window closes somewhere around sixteen weeks. Inside it, a puppy files new things under normal with very little effort. Outside it, new things get filed under threat by default and take months of work to move. That is the whole reason the window matters.

Quantity is the wrong target

A puppy who meets forty dogs and is frightened by nine of them has learned that dogs are unpredictable. A puppy who watches six dogs from a comfortable distance, eats something nice each time, and never gets rushed, has learned that dogs are boring and fine. Boring and fine is the goal.

Watch the puppy, not the checklist

Loose body, willing to take food, able to move toward the new thing on their own means you are at the right distance. Tucked tail, refusing food they normally love, freezing, or trying to get behind you means you are too close. Add distance rather than encouragement.

The list nobody writes down

Umbrellas, wheelie bins, men in hats, bicycles, hardwood floors, stairs, hair dryers, people using crutches, skateboards, car rides that end somewhere pleasant, being handled around the paws and ears, and being alone for short periods. Vet clinics belong on this list, which is why our intro visit exists and costs nothing.

Vaccination and risk

The old advice to keep a puppy inside until fully vaccinated cost more dogs their homes through behavior problems than parvovirus ever did. The current position of the American Veterinary Society of Animal Behavior is that puppies can and should start socialisation from seven weeks, on clean surfaces, with vaccinated dogs, one week after their first vaccine round. Talk to your veterinarian about the risk where you live before you plan an outing.

Why a matted coat gets shaved, not brushed

Grooming. Written by Amara Osei, NCMG. Last updated 20 February 2026.

A mat is not a tangle. It is a dense felted pad, welded to the skin, that pulls every time the animal moves. Working a mat out with a brush takes hours, and every one of those minutes hurts. Skin under a tight mat is often already inflamed, sometimes broken, and occasionally hiding a hot spot or a foreign body.

How we decide

If a comb will not pass between the mat and the skin, it comes off with clippers. That is the rule, and it is the same rule every reputable salon uses. We call you before we do it, because a shaved dog looks shocking if you were not expecting it, and we will send you a photo if that helps.

Why the coat looks strange afterwards

A shaved double coat grows back unevenly for a few months, and in some breeds the texture changes permanently. That is a real cost, and it is still better than leaving a pad of felt pulling on skin. Preventing the next one is the actual answer.

Preventing the next one

Line brushing, not surface brushing. Part the coat, brush from the skin outward, work in sections. Ten minutes three times a week beats an hour once a month. Pay attention to friction points: behind the ears, under the collar, in the armpits, and where a harness sits. If your dog swims or gets wet, dry and brush before the coat dries flat.

The four things older cats hide until it is late

Feline care. Reviewed by Dr. Priya Raghunathan, DVM. Last updated 8 January 2026.

Cats evolved to conceal weakness, and they are very good at it. By the time a cat looks unwell, the condition is usually well established. These four account for most of what we find on senior screening panels.

Chronic kidney disease

Drinking more, urinating more, gradual weight loss, and a coat that stops looking cared for. Blood and urine testing catches it years before those signs appear, which is why we screen annually from age seven and twice yearly from eleven.

Hyperthyroidism

A cat that eats constantly and still loses weight, becomes vocal at night, or seems restless. It is common, it is treatable, and untreated it damages the heart.

Dental pain

Cats with painful mouths keep eating, because the alternative is starving. Look for chewing on one side, dropping food, drooling, or a cat that has quietly stopped grooming.

Arthritis

Around ninety percent of cats over twelve have arthritic change on radiographs, and almost none of them limp. They stop jumping onto the counter, sleep somewhere lower, or start missing the litter box because climbing into it hurts.

If you recognise your cat here, book an exam rather than searching further.

None of these can be diagnosed from a website, and all four are more manageable when they are found early.

Tick season on the Front Range

Preventive care. Reviewed by Dr. Priya Raghunathan, DVM. Last updated 14 March 2026.

Rocky Mountain wood ticks become active as soon as the snow clears at lower elevations, usually late March, and stay active into July. Higher trails run later. Colorado has fewer tick borne diseases than the north east, but Rocky Mountain spotted fever and tick paralysis both occur here.

Checking properly

Run your fingertips slowly against the lie of the coat, not with it. Ticks favour warm thin skin: inside the ears, under the collar, the armpits, between the toes, and around the tail base. Do it the same evening as the hike, because a tick that is removed within 24 hours rarely transmits anything.

Removing one

Fine tipped tweezers, grip as close to the skin as you can, pull straight out with steady pressure. Do not twist, do not burn it, do not smother it in petroleum jelly. Those methods make the tick regurgitate, which is exactly what you are trying to avoid. Clean the site afterwards and note the date.

Prevention

Monthly oral or topical preventives work well and are prescription items in Colorado, so they need a current veterinary relationship. Ask us which suits your dog, since the right choice depends on age, weight, whether there are cats in the house, and whether the dog swims.

Separation distress is not spite

Behavior. Written by Dana Whitcomb, CPDT-KA. Last updated 27 May 2026.

A dog that destroys a door frame while you are out is not making a point about being left. Dogs do not hold grudges across hours and then act on them. What you are looking at is panic, and panic responds to a completely different approach than misbehaviour does.

Confirm what is actually happening

Set a camera before you change anything. Distress usually starts within fifteen minutes of departure and centres on exits. A dog that settles for two hours and then chews a shoe is bored, not panicking, and boredom is a much easier problem.

Graduated absences

Find the length of absence your dog handles calmly, even if that is eleven seconds, and work from there. Leave, return before any distress starts, repeat. Extend by small increments, vary the length so the dog cannot predict it, and never push through a session where the dog panicked. Progress is measured in weeks.

Do not skip the veterinary step

Severe separation distress is often helped considerably by medication, and withholding it while a dog panics daily for six months is not the kinder option. Our veterinarians and trainers work the case together, and we refer to a board certified veterinary behaviorist when a dog needs one.

Honest expectations.

This work takes months, progress is not linear, and some dogs need long term management rather than a cure. We will tell you where your dog sits rather than sell you a package.
